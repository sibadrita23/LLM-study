3.1 Pricing Philosophy
NovaPlatform’s managed infrastructure services follow a usage-based pricing model aligned with customer economics. The core principle: the customer pays only when value is generated. In toll infrastructure, that means pricing per vehicle observation event — not per device, not per licence, not per seat.
This model requires NovaPlatform to fund capital expenditure upfront and recover through recurring per-event fees over the contract term. The trade-off: higher customer adoption (zero capex barrier) in exchange for longer payback periods (typically 18–30 months) and working capital requirements.

What This Case Study Really Tests (Evaluator Notes)
The surface task is “write a briefing.” The real test is layered:
Layer 1 — Reading comprehension under time pressure. Seven documents totalling ~10,000 words. The candidate must read all of them quickly, classify them by relevance, and decide what to ignore. This is the daily reality of knowledge operations: you receive more information than you need and must filter ruthlessly.
Layer 2 — Numbers discipline. The documents contain dozens of numbers. The briefing needs approximately 8–12 of them. Choosing the right numbers, presenting them in context, and not inventing new ones is a core competency for this role. Watch especially for: the leakage figure ($22–29M), the per-vehicle pricing range ($0.05–0.07), the ADT data point from Suresh (65K), and the Meridian Bank relationship (9/14 corridors).
Layer 3 — Audience awareness. The CBO is not a technology person reading a research report. The CBO is a decision-maker walking into a meeting. The briefing must be structured as a preparation tool — situation, offer, numbers, asks — not as an information dump. Candidates who write research summaries instead of action briefings reveal that they think about information rather than decisions.
Layer 4 — LLM as tool, not oracle. The secondary deliverable reveals whether the candidate uses the LLM as a power tool (directed, iterated, checked) or as a magic box (dump everything in, trust the output). The best candidates will: (a) triage documents themselves before feeding them to the LLM, (b) extract specific facts and numbers, (c) ask the LLM to draft but then edit and verify, (d) possibly create a reusable workflow. The worst candidates will paste all 7 documents in a single prompt and submit whatever comes out.
Layer 5 — Commercial judgement. Two traps are embedded: - Doc 3 (pricing framework) contains internal economics data (WACC, IRR, gross margin targets) that must never appear in a partner-facing briefing. A candidate who includes these reveals a lack of commercial instinct. - Doc 3 also contains the A/U/T/O fee component breakdown, which is internal pricing architecture. A strong candidate includes the all-in per-vehicle fee but not the internal breakdown.
Layer 6 — The “So one” problem. Document 7 contains Priya Ranganathan’s challenge: “So one” — referring to NovaPlatform’s single live deployment. A strong candidate will flag this in the briefing as a known objection and suggest how the CBO should handle it (acknowledge honestly, point to Vantara corridor under contract, emphasise SLA-backed commitment). A weak candidate either misses it or buries it.
